- **Module 24 — Inventory & Assets** (2026-08-02): the school's store and the things it owns — what it stocks, what arrived, what went out, who is holding it, and what it paid. **The module turns on one distinction and everything else falls out of it**: a CONSUMABLE is *counted* (the ledger's balance is the truth, and nothing tracks an individual biro) while an ASSET is *identified* (every unit gets a tag, a location and a custodian, and "how many do we have" is answered by counting rows). That is why receiving ten chairs and ten reams of paper are two different events, why `canIssue` refuses an asset **structurally** — no permission reaches it — and why the asset register and the stock report are different screens. **The ledger is the only place a balance exists**: there is deliberately no `qty` column on `items`, because a stored quantity is a second source of truth that drifts from the movements beneath it and then has to be "corrected" by the very screen that broke it. `balance_after` is a **running** total written under a `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the *item* (not on the ledger — the row does not exist yet, and locking "the last entry" races with an insert), which is what lets an item ledger show a running balance without an O(n²) scan and what makes concurrent issues safe; `recordMany` sorts by item id first, because two clerks issuing the same two items in opposite orders is a textbook deadlock that only appears under load. Every movement is **one-sided** (`qty_in` or `qty_out`, never a signed `qty` — the M20 `chk_voucher_entries_one_sided` shape applied to things), and **`balance_after >= 0` is a CHECK**: a school cannot issue what it does not have, and the database says so even when a future write path forgets to ask. **An adjustment is expressed as the movement it implies, not as a new balance** — a count that finds 8 where the ledger says 12 writes `qty_out: 4`, which keeps every row a *movement* so the column always adds up, and is what makes §8's count-sheet wizard a list of differences rather than of overwrites (the remark records `ledger 12 → counted 8`, because "corrected to 8" means nothing a year later). §8's box→pcs conversion is a **stored fact**: a purchase line carries `qty` as typed, a *snapshot* of the pack size, and `base_qty` pinned by `chk_purchase_items_shape` to `qty × pack_size`, so the ledger only ever speaks base units and the two can never drift. **An asset tag is never reused and an item code is** — `uq_asset_units_tag` ignores `deleted_at` (the M07/M09/M23 rule; it is a sticker on a projector) while `uq_items_code` is live-rows scoped (the M25 plate rule; it is a label the school chooses), and the two sitting one table apart is the clearest statement of that distinction the codebase has. **One shape answers "who has it"** for both the issue recipient and the asset custodian — `(kind, department | person | room)`, pinned by a CHECK on each table — because answering it two ways would give the desk and the register two vocabularies for one fact; §8's shared-custody case is why DEPARTMENT exists at all.
